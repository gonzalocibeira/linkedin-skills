## Find the story

Use the topic or draft the author supplied. For discovery, look through the current user's available conversations, project notes and recent drafts within the requested period (default to the past week). Look for a problem they encountered, a decision they made, or an outcome they can describe firsthand. Check whether a similar story was discussed, drafted or published before pitching it again. For current events, verify the original announcement and date; use a personal angle only when the author has expressed it.

For ideas, return up to three distinct one-line pitches. For a named topic, draft directly. For a request to both find and write a story, pick the strongest supported angle and finish the post. If history is unavailable, use the supplied material; ask for one recent event or example if the request otherwise requires guessing.

## Match the author

Start with the current brief. Use this author's own posts, explicit preferences and past edits to infer language variety, register, rhythm, technical depth, humor, formatting and hashtag placement. Give more weight to their wording and corrections than to assistant-written drafts. Treat a one-off creative choice as local to that post. If no samples are available, use the brief's language and a plain, concise style. Keep personal examples and preferences in the current user's context; keep shared instructions reusable.

Open with the most telling detail. For a project made for someone, the person's need can explain the project in a sentence. For a technical result, a verified before-and-after can make a better opening than a general claim. Choose a structure that suits the story. Give readers enough context to understand what changed and why the author cared.

Check names, numbers, credit and completion status. Describe experiments at their actual stage. Use fictional examples for sensitive work details and honor the author's public-sharing preferences. Keep private context used to infer voice out of the public copy unless the author intends to tell that story.

## Revise and deliver

Preserve approved lines and the scope of the edit. When a draft feels generic, replace an abstraction with a supported detail. When asked to shorten it, remove setup and repetition first. Return paste-ready copy and keep any source notes outside it. For a requested accompanying asset, pass the final copy and relevant art direction to linkedin-visual when available.
